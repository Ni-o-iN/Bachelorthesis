Coffee ants, scientifically known as Azteca sericeasur, are a species of ant commonly found in coffee plantations in Central and South America. These ants have a symbiotic relationship with coffee plants and certain types of scale insects. The ants protect the scale insects from predators and, in return, feed on the honeydew produced by these insects.

This mutualistic relationship benefits both the ants and the scale insects, but it can be detrimental to coffee plants as the scale insects feed on the plant sap, potentially reducing the plant's health and productivity.

Coffee ants build their nests in the hollow stems of coffee plants, providing them with a safe and stable environment. These nests can house thousands of ants, including workers, soldiers, and reproductive individuals.

The presence of coffee ants can influence the ecological balance of coffee plantations, affecting other insect populations and plant health.

Researchers are studying the impact of coffee ants on coffee production to develop sustainable pest management strategies. Understanding the behavior and ecology of these ants can help in creating methods to control their population and mitigate their negative effects on coffee plants.
