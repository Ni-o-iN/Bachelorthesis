A paper eclipse is a creative and educational project that simulates a solar or lunar eclipse using paper and other simple materials. This activity is often used in classrooms to help students understand the mechanics of eclipses. By constructing models of the Sun, Moon, and Earth, students can visualize how these celestial bodies align during an eclipse.

To create a paper eclipse, you typically need colored paper, scissors, glue, and a light source.

For a solar eclipse model, you cut out circles representing the Sun and Moon. The Sun is usually larger and yellow, while the Moon is smaller and gray.

By positioning the Moon in front of the Sun and shining a light behind them, you can demonstrate how the Moon blocks the Sun's light, casting a shadow on the Earth.

For a lunar eclipse model, you need circles for the Earth and Moon. The Earth is placed between the Sun and the Moon.

When the Earth blocks the Sun's light, it casts a shadow on the Moon, causing a lunar eclipse.

This hands-on activity helps students grasp the concept of shadows and the relative positions of celestial bodies during an eclipse.

Paper eclipses are not only educational but also fun and engaging. They encourage creativity and provide a tangible way to explore astronomical phenomena.

This project can be adapted for various age groups and educational levels, making it a versatile tool for teaching astronomy.
